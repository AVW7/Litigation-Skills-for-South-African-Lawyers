# Negligence Test

$$ \text{Liability for Negligence} = \text{Foreseeability of Harm} \land \text{Preventability of Harm} \land \text{Failure to Take Reasonable Steps} $$

## Symbol-by-symbol

| symbol | meaning | role |
|--------|---------|------|
| $\text{Foreseeability}$ | Whether a diligens paterfamilias (reasonable person) would foresee the reasonable possibility of conduct causing harm. | Requirement 1 |
| $\text{Preventability}$ | Whether a reasonable person would take reasonable steps to guard against such harm. | Requirement 2 |
| $\text{Failure to Prevent}$ | Whether the defendant failed to take such steps. | Requirement 3 |

## What it computes

Negligence in the South African law of delict, establishing the fault element for damages claims.

## Why this form

> [!tip] Judicial Origin
> Established in *Kruger v Coetzee* 1966 (2) SA 428 (A). It splits negligence into three distinct, cumulative elements that must be proved by the plaintiff on a balance of probabilities.

> [!example] Worked Legal Application
> **Facts:** Defendant drives through a red traffic light at an intersection and collides with Plaintiff.
> 1. **Foreseeability:** A reasonable driver would foresee that driving through a red light could cause a collision and harm to others.
> 2. **Preventability:** A reasonable driver would prevent this by stopping at the red light.
> 3. **Failure:** The defendant failed to stop.
> **Conclusion:** The defendant was negligent.

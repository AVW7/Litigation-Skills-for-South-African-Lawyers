# Urgent Application Test

$$ \text{Urgency relief} = \text{Explicit Urgency Circumstances} \land \text{Absence of Substantial Redress in Due Course} $$

## Symbol-by-symbol

| symbol                 | meaning                                                                                            | role                        |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| $\text{Circumstances}$ | Explicit details showing why the matter is urgent and cannot wait.                                 | Rule 6(12)(b) Requirement 1 |
| $\text{Redress}$       | Explicit reasons why the applicant will not obtain substantial redress at a hearing in due course. | Rule 6(12)(b) Requirement 2 |

## What it computes

Whether a matter qualifies to be heard as an urgent application in the Motion Court, bypassing standard time frames.

## Why this form

> [!tip] Rationale
> High Court Rule 6(12) requires both elements to be set out under oath in the founding affidavit. A failure to prove either will result in the matter being struck from the roll with costs.
<!-- -->
> [!example] Worked Legal Application
> **Facts:** Applicant seeks an interdict to stop a developer from demolishing a heritage building tomorrow.
>
> 1. **Circumstances:** Demolition is scheduled for tomorrow at 08:00; heritage authority has not intervened.
> 2. **No Redress:** If heard in the normal course (months away), the building will already be destroyed, making any subsequent order useless.
>
> **Conclusion:** Urgency established.

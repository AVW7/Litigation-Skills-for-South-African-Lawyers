# Trial Advocacy & Courtroom Strategies

### 4.1 Chronology & Case Theory
* **Fact Analysis:** Arrange all facts and documents in strict chronological order. It aids witness memory, ensures completeness, and helps a compelling case theory emerge.
* **Theory of the Case:** Start early with a preliminary theory and refine it as information accumulates. Do not cling to a hopeless theory.

### 4.2 Trial Phases
1. **Opening Statement:** Outlines what the party intends to prove.
2. **Examination-in-Chief:**
   * Elicit evidence in a logical, chronological sequence.
   * **Constraint:** No leading questions are permitted on material or disputed facts.
   * *Refreshing memory:* A witness may refresh their memory from notes only if the notes were made at or near the time of the event, while facts were fresh in their mind, and with permission from the court.
3. **Cross-Examination:**
   * **The Duty to Put Your Version:** You must put your client's version to the opposing witness on points where your own witnesses will contradict them. If you fail to do so, you cannot later argue that the witness's evidence on that point should be rejected.
   * Only put parts of the version the witness is reasonably expected to know or comment on.
   * *Restrictions:* Focus cross-examination on the merits (testing reliability of observations and memory) and credibility (honesty, inconsistencies).
4. **Re-Examination:**
   * Strictly limited to clarifying matters raised during cross-examination.
   * No new areas may be explored without leave of the court.
5. **Closing Argument:**
   * Summarize the proven facts, apply the legal principles, and address why the relief in the prayer should be granted.

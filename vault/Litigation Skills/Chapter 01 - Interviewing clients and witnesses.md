## 1. The Structure of a New Client Interview

The primary goal of a client interview is to elicit information that is **relevant**, **complete**, and **chronological**. A lawyer should follow a structured 8-stage consultation process:

### Stage 1: Meeting & Pleasantries

First impressions are lasting. Meet the client in the reception area promptly. Do not wait behind your desk or send a secretary. Escort the client to your office, using the walk to break the ice with light small-talk (weather, parking, etc.).

* **Office Environment:** Ensure your office is comforting (muted colors, plants, family photos rather than imposing legal volumes). Seat the client in a comfortable chair away from harsh sunlight. Clear your desk of other work to show undivided attention. Never offer alcohol; offer tea, coffee, or water.

### Stage 2: Initial Problem & Goal Identification

Invite the client to tell their story in their own words. Listen actively to identify their perceived problem and desired outcome.

* **Note-taking rule:** Maintain direct eye contact and show empathy. **Do not take notes or review files** at this stage, as it can distance the client.
* *Example (Mrs Anne Smith):* The client arrives in distress; her husband was killed in a collision, she and her children are injured, hospital bills are mounting, and the executor refuses to release life policy funds. As a lawyer, you immediately identify potential claims: delictual Road Accident Fund (RAF) loss of support claim, RAF personal injury claim, common law Aquilian action for car damage, insurance cessions, and deceased estate maintenance.

### Stage 3: Dealing with Preliminary Matters

Before gathering detailed facts, establish mutual expectations:

1. **Confidentiality:** Reassure the client that everything disclosed is protected by attorney-client privilege.
2. **Fees:** Explain fee structures clearly. Mention that the initial consultation is free in accordance with Law Society practices.
3. **Conflicts of Interest:** Advise that you will check for potential conflicts.
4. **Note-taking Permission:** Ask the client's permission to begin taking notes.

### Stage 4: Chronological Fact Finding

Cases are decided on facts. Explain the importance of obtaining a detailed chronological history and securing relevant documents (marriage/birth certificates, payslips, tax returns, executor details, medical/hospital invoices).

* **Questioning Techniques:**
  * **Open questions:** Inquisitive rather than suggestive. E.g., *"Please tell me everything you remember of the collision."* Used to introduce a general topic.
  * **Closed questions:** Seek specific details. E.g., *"At what speed were you travelling?"* Often uses the **piggy-backing** technique (grafting the question onto a prior answer).
  * **Leading questions:** Suggest the answer. E.g., *"You applied the brakes, didn't you?"* **Do not use leading questions** in the initial stages, as clients tend to agree with the suggestion rather than providing their true recollection.
* **Funnelling Technique:** Start with broad open questions, then employ closed, non-leading questions to narrow down the focus and flush out specific details.
* **Types of Evidence:**
  * **Direct evidence:** Direct observation (e.g., eyewitness testimony).
  * **Circumstantial evidence:** Surrounding circumstances suggesting a fact (e.g., debris on the road indicating point of impact).
  * **Reliability evidence:** Facts that strengthen or weaken witness testimony (e.g., visibility conditions, bias, prior inconsistent statements).

### Stage 5: Fact Analysis & Case Theory Development

Start early with a preliminary theory of the case. The theory must be coherent, comprehensive, and accommodate all known facts, including adverse ones. The **proof-making model** of fact analysis maps the case across five analytical stages:

1. Area of Law $\rightarrow$ 2. Cause of Action $\rightarrow$ 3. Legal Elements (*Facta Probanda*) $\rightarrow$ 4. Propositions of Fact $\rightarrow$ 5. Evidence (*Facta Probantia*)

### Stage 6: Giving Preliminary Advice

Structure advice around the client's questions: claims available, timelines, costs, and strengths.

* > [!WARNING] Be Conservative
  > Avoid overly optimistic predictions. Unqualified advice can come back to haunt you. If you are unsure about the merits, state that you need to conduct legal research or consult counsel before providing a final opinion.

### Stage 7: Concluding the Interview

Ensure all concerns are addressed. Obtain explicit written authority (e.g., executing a Power of Attorney or medical record access consents). Explain the next steps clearly so the client knows exactly what to expect and when. Walk the client to the exit and shake hands.

### Stage 8: After the Interview

Immediately write up detailed interview notes and draft a formal client letter summarizing the consultation, advice given, and agreed-upon action plan. Record time spent and diarize next steps.

## 2. Interviewing Witnesses

To prevent the contamination of evidence, follow these strict safeguards:

* **Separate Interviews:** Never interview a potential witness in the presence of the client or other witnesses.
* **No Leading Questions:** Restrict questioning to open and non-leading closed formats, just as you would do during examination-in-chief in court.
* **Written Record:** Take written notes of the witness's version, read it back for confirmation, and request their signature.
* **Advocate & Attorney Team:** An advocate should only interview witnesses with the instructing attorney present. The attorney should draft and secure the signed statement.

### Opposing Witnesses (GCB Rule 4.3.1)

Witnesses do not "belong" to any party; you are entitled to interview anyone with information. However:

1. **Pre-Trial:** If the opponent has subpoenaed, interviewed, or taken a statement from the witness, you must notify the opponent of your intention to interview them. The opponent cannot attend or block the interview.
2. **Post-Testimony:** If the witness has already testified in the proceedings, the interview may not occur without the opponent's lawyer being present, unless they decline to attend.

## 3. Interviewing in Criminal Practice

### Prosecution Protocols

* **Complainant Treatment:** Treat the victim with professional courtesy and empathy. Do not judge or express personal belief; remain objective. Explain the courtroom procedure before calling them.
* **Sex Crimes (CPA Section 227):** Section 227 of the Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977 protects the complainant's privacy by rendering evidence of prior sexual conduct inadmissible unless the court grants leave. The prosecutor must conduct sensitive, structured pre-trial interviews to anticipate defence applications:
  * Ensure private setting with a support person present.
  * Use active listening and precise, respectful terminology.
  * Avoid making moral or ethical judgments.
* **Minors:** Interview only with a guardian or relative present. Assess if testifying will cause undue stress and, if so, apply for intermediary assistance under Section 170A of the CPA.

### Defence Protocols

* **Accused Consultations:** Conduct consultations in private (in the absence of family members or funding parties) to protect attorney-client privilege.
* **Written Records:** Because accused clients often blame counsel for litigation failures:
  * Record all advice given in writing and obtain the client's signed acknowledgement.
  * Obtain written, signed instructions for all decisions (e.g., pleading guilty/not guilty, testifying or remaining silent).
* **Prosecution Witnesses (GCB Rule 4.3.2):** Defence counsel **must not** interview a prosecution witness (one who has given a statement to the police or is subpoenaed by the state) without the express permission of the prosecutor/attorney-general, subject to any conditions they impose. If refused, counsel may apply for leave of the court.

## 4. Interpreter Protocol

When conducting consultations through an interpreter:

* **Convey Meaning:** An interpreter conveys the *meaning and context*, not just word-for-word translation.
* **Briefing:** Brief the interpreter on the case background and provide a list of technical/legal terms in advance.
* **Communication Tips:**
  * Speak directly to the witness, maintaining eye contact with them rather than the interpreter.
  * Ask short, simple questions using clear, direct speech. Avoid convoluted, double-barrelled questions.
  * Watch the body language of both the witness and the interpreter.
  * Do not interrupt the interpreter; give them the time and space to communicate.

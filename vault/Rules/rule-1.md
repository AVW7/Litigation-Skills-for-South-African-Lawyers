# Professional Conduct & Ethical Duties

In the South African litigation process, counsel (whether an advocate, attorney-advocate, or prosecutor) serves two interests: **the court** and **the client**.

> [!warning] Precedence of Duties
> Counsel has a higher duty to the court than to the client. If a conflict arises (e.g., a client refuses to allow counsel to disclose adverse authority), counsel **must** refuse to act or continue acting.

---

### 🏛️ Duty to the Court (Do Not Mislead)
* **Merits of the Case:** Do not advise the client to pursue litigation unless there is a reasonable case. (See [[Chapter 02 - Advising and counselling clients|Chapter 2]])
* **Honesty in Fact and Law:** Do not knowingly or recklessly deceive the court on facts or law (*"In matters of fact, it is counsel's duty not to mislead the court"*).
* **Adducing Evidence:** Do not tender evidence known to be false.
* **Arguments:** Do not present specious, frivolous, or obviously untenable points.
* **Legal Authority:** Do not withhold adverse legal authorities from the court. Always present both helpful and adverse precedents.

---

### 🤝 Duty to the Client (Provide Fearless Advocacy)
* **Objective Advice:** Provide objective, practical advice, even if it is not what the client wishes to hear.
* **Client Interest:** Strive to protect the client's legitimate interests with all your skill.
* **Presentation of Case:** Fearlessly stand on the right to present the client's case fully at every stage of the proceedings.

---

### ⚖️ Prosecutor's Additional Duty
* **Fairness:** Prosecutors must be fair and objective.
* **Disclosure:** Prosecutors must not withhold evidence that may exonerate the accused, and must make such evidence available to the defence.

# Rules of Pleadings & Drafting

Core guidelines for drafting substantive pleadings and applications in South African practice.

---

### 📝 3.1 Substantive Pleadings Rules
* **Facta Probanda vs. Facta Probantia:**
  * Plead only the **facta probanda** (the material facts which must be proved to establish the cause of action). (See [[Page-47|Page 47]])
  * Exclude the **facta probantia** (the evidence by which those material facts are proved).
* **The Prayer:**
  * Every statement of claim (or particulars of claim) must conclude with a clear **prayer** specifying the exact relief sought. A pleading without a prayer is deficient and excipient (vague and embarrassing, or failing to disclose a cause of action). (See [[Page-54|Page 54]])
* **Drafting the Plea:**
  * The defendant must answer every allegation in the plaintiff’s statement of claim line-by-line. (See [[Page-66|Page 66]])
  * Each allegation must be admitted, denied, or confessed and avoided. Any allegation not denied is deemed admitted.

---

### 🚫 3.2 Exceptions & Striking Out
* **Exceptions:** Taken in terms of Rule 23 if a pleading lacks averments necessary to sustain an action/defence, or if it is vague and embarrassing. (See [[Chapter 09 - Drafting exceptions, applications to strike out and objections to a charge|Chapter 9]])
* **Striking Out:** Used to remove scandalous, vexatious, or irrelevant matter from pleadings.

---

### 📑 3.3 Motion Court (Applications)
* *"The Motion Court is a paper court. But it is no paper tiger!"* (See [[Chapter 22 - Motion Court|Chapter 22]])
* **Affidavits:** Applications are decided entirely on affidavits: Founding, Answering, and Replying.
* **Urgency:** To succeed in an urgent application, counsel must set forth explicitly:
  1. The circumstances rendering the matter urgent.
  2. The reasons why the applicant could not be afforded substantial redress at a hearing in due course.

## Prosecution witnesses

Anticipate that the prosecutor will call Irene Delamere and perhaps also Sgt Reddy, to give evidence in accordance with their statements.

THEMES for CROSS-EXAMINING Ms Delamere:

Store policy to prosecute.

Similarity between bags, in weight, texture etc. (Blindfold test? Risky.).

Did not keep observation all the time - her statement is silent on how the accused's backpack came to be on the floor, and she told Reddy it was found later.

Price tag on arm strap of bag.

Did not see price tag being concealed.

Accused acted "openly".

PUT:

1. Accused said: "I am sorry, it's a mistake."
2. She said that it was "unfortunately" the store's policy to prosecute.
3. The accused did not intend to steal the backpack - he thought he was carrying his own backpack.

THEMES for CROSS-EXAMINING Sgt Reddy:

Confirm what Delamere told him - the accused's backpack was "found" etc.

PUT:

Explained very carefully to the accused that he did not have to make a statement.

## Draft opening statement

This is a case of an unfortunate but understandable mistake rather than deliberate wrongdoing.

The accused suffers from colour-blindness and by mistake took the store's backpack thinking it was his own.

The issue is therefore the *mens rea* element of theft.

Mr Graham cannot distinguish between colours in the brown and red range.

He went to the store to buy a new bag as his old backpack was torn. He had a newspaper in the backpack. He had kept it because it contained job advertisements he intended to follow up.

He wanted a new bag rather than a backpack, as he thought a bag might be more in keeping with the position he was seeking.

He looked at some backpacks. He put his own on the floor and mistakenly slung the store's backpack over his shoulder when he went to look at some bags.

When he found nothing to his liking, he left the store with the store's backpack still over his shoulder.

[[[Page-296|page 491]]]

It was only when he was stopped outside that he realised he had the store's backpack and not his own.

He apologised for his mistake immediately but, unfortunately, it is the store's policy to prosecute in all cases.

I intend to call 2/3 witnesses.

The accused will give evidence as I have outlined already.

His family doctor, Dr Ivan Stone, will give evidence confirming that the accused is colour-blind, and will explain that the accused cannot distinguish between those colours normally sighted people see as reds and browns.

(Friend/schoolmate?)

The accused did not intend to take the store's backpack. He did not even realise he had it. He therefore lacked the necessary *mens rea* for theft.

## Draft closing argument

The issue is *animus furandi*.

The burden of proof is on the Prosecution. It has to prove that a mistake such as that explained by the accused and Dr Stone **did not occur**. The test is whether the accused's version can reasonably be true. (See *S v Luther en"n*

If the client after considering these matters and your advice should agree to mediation, your role as lawyer is not over yet. Your client will need advice and assistance throughout the procedures that follow upon the decision.

A properly structured mediation will usually include the -

- [ ] the agreement by the parties to resolve their dispute through mediation;
- [ ] the appointment of a mediator;
- [ ] a preliminary conference where the parties will discuss, among other things, the role of the mediator and their lawyers, confidentiality, the issues, documents, information and reports to be exchanged, and the date, time and venue of the mediation;
- [ ] the exchange of documents, information and reports, if agreed at the preliminary meeting;
- [ ] the hearing; and
- [ ] implementation of the outcome, if the mediation has been successful.

There is no prescribed procedure to be adhered to at the mediation. Nevertheless, an experienced mediator will probably follow a staged approach:

- [ ] the mediator makes an opening statement, explaining how the process will be conducted, what standards of behaviour will be expected of the parties, and encourage an attitude conducive to a resolution of the dispute;
- [ ] the parties in turn are given an opportunity to state their key concerns briefly;
- [ ] the mediator ensures that the parties understand the issues and sets an agenda for the discussion;
- [ ] the mediator and the parties go into session, discussing the issues in the order set previously;
- [ ] if deemed advisable, the mediator meets with each of the parties separately for confidential discussions;
- [ ] the parties negotiate under the guidance of the mediator; and
- [ ] any agreement the parties reach is reduced to writing. If the parties cannot agree, they record that fact and may then have to proceed to another form of dispute resolution.

Lawyers have an important role to play at various stages of the resolution of the dispute by mediation. It is of the essence in the mediation process that the parties will negotiate with each other with the purpose of finding a mutually acceptable settlement. Thus, the skills, procedures, protocols and ethics of the negotiation process will also be brought to bear on the mediation process. The preparation, planning and structure of the mediation will resemble that of a negotiation. Such differences as there may be are necessitated by the fact that the negotiations are conducted under the guidance of the mediator.

You will be involved in the following stages of the mediation process:

- [ ] In the initial stage when you are consulted, you should investigate the matter in order to determine whether the client has a claim or defence and whether the dispute is suitable to be dealt with through the mediation process. After the client has been advised on the benefits of mediation and mediation has been decided upon, you should seek the agreement of the other party (through his or her lawyer, if one has been instructed), that the parties should try to resolve their dispute through mediation.
- [ ] You should then advise the client on the terms of an appropriate mediation agreement and draft or settle it. It is usually agreed (in the mediation agreement) that all [[[Page-37|page 52]]] concessions and discussions in the mediation will be without prejudice and will be kept confidential. Such concessions and discussions will therefore enjoy privilege from disclosure in subsequent proceedings. Once the mediation agreement has been signed by the parties, you should prepare the client for the mediation. This preparation is done the same way as for a negotiation, except that the client should be made aware of the mediator's role. You have to keep the client fully advised throughout the mediation process but the client will have to make his or her own decisions. Mediation, like other forms of ADR, can only be conducted properly if the client is fully prepared as if for trial. This ensures that the client has a full understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of the case, the facts and documents, the important issues, as well as the risks involved, and has a broad understanding of the legal principles involved.
